
# Analysis of Wikipedia's Organizing System

## Introduction

For this analysis, I chose **Wikipedia**, one of the largest public knowledge bases on the internet. Wikipedia contains information about millions of topics, including people, places, historical events, science, technology, entertainment, and current events. Its organizing system allows users to search for specific information while also discovering related topics through links and categories.

---

## 1. Primary Resources Being Organized

The primary resources organized by Wikipedia are **articles and the information contained within them**. Each article focuses on a particular topic, person, place, event, concept, or organization. For example, an article about the _Apollo 11_ mission contains information about the mission's crew, launch, landing, and historical significance.

Wikipedia also organizes supporting resources such as **images, references, citations, tables, and external links**. These resources help provide additional information and evidence related to an article.

> Wikipedia is designed to connect large amounts of information so users can move from one topic to another.

---

## 2. Primary Interactions

The main interactions Wikipedia supports are **searching, researching, browsing, and fact-checking**. Users can search for a specific topic using the search bar or browse through categories and links.

For example, someone researching the **history of video games** could start with a general article and then follow links to specific consoles, companies, games, or important events. Users can also check the references at the bottom of an article to find the sources used to support its information.

Another important interaction is **discovery**. Hyperlinks within articles make it easy for users to explore topics they may not have originally intended to research.

---

## 3. Classification System

Wikipedia uses a combination of **hierarchical and faceted classification**. Its category system provides a hierarchical structure where broad categories can contain more specific subcategories. For example, a topic can be organized under categories related to _Science_, _Technology_, or _History_, with additional subcategories underneath them.

Wikipedia also uses facets such as **date, location, occupation, subject, and topic** to help describe and organize resources from different perspectives.

This system is effective because Wikipedia serves a very broad audience. Users can either search directly for an article or browse through categories when they are unsure of exactly what information they need. The combination of different organizational methods makes the large collection easier to navigate.

---

## 4. Relationships Between Resources

Relationships between Wikipedia resources are primarily represented through **hyperlinks, categories, and "See also" sections**. Articles contain links to other Wikipedia pages, allowing users to quickly move between related subjects.

For example, an article about a musician may link to their albums, bands, songs, and related artists. The **"See also"** section can provide additional related topics that may not be directly discussed in the article.

Categories create another type of relationship by grouping articles that share common characteristics. References and citations also connect an article to external sources.

---

## Conclusion

Overall, Wikipedia organizes a massive collection of information using **articles, categories, hyperlinks, references, and multiple classification methods**. Its system supports searching, research, browsing, and discovery. The combination of hierarchical categories and multiple descriptive facets allows users to approach information in different ways, making Wikipedia a useful example of how digital information can be organized for a large and diverse audience.
