## AI Music Metadata

**Music metadata** is information that describes a song or audio file. Instead of only having the actual audio, metadata gives a music library details that help identify, search, and organize each track. Common metadata includes the song title, artist, album, genre, release year, BPM, and mood.

### Common Music Metadata

- **Artist:** The person or group who created the song.
- **Song Title:** The name of the track.
- **Genre:** The type of music, such as house, EDM, hip-hop, rock, or pop.
- **Mood:** Describes the feeling or atmosphere of a song, such as energetic, chill, happy, dark, or emotional.
- **BPM:** Stands for _beats per minute_ and measures the tempo of a song. This is especially useful for DJs when choosing songs that mix well together.
- **Year:** The year the song or album was released.
- **Album:** The album, EP, or release that the song belongs to.
- **Track Number:** Shows where a song appears within an album.
- **Key:** The musical key of the song, which can also be useful when creating DJ mixes.

### How MixMind Uses Metadata

**MixMind uses metadata to turn a large music collection into an easier-to-search and organize library.** When songs are added, MixMind can use information such as artist, genre, mood, BPM, year, and album to create useful categories and make songs easier to find.

For example, a DJ could search for **house songs from 2020–2024 with a BPM around 125 that have an energetic mood**. Instead of manually searching through hundreds or thousands of files, MixMind can use the metadata to narrow the collection down.

MixMind can also combine different pieces of metadata. A song could be organized as:

> **Artist:** Fred again..  
> **Genre:** Electronic / House  
> **Mood:** Energetic  
> **BPM:** 125  
> **Year:** 2024  
> **Album:** Example Album

This makes it easier for users to find the **right song for the right moment** while still allowing them to review and change how their music is organized. Metadata is particularly useful because music platforms already rely on this type of information to identify and categorize tracks.
